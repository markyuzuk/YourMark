The Origin Story

How a commitment to culture sparked a movement to transform clinical research.

The Foundation: Culture First

Matt Kiernan learned a fundamental truth early in his career: companies built on strong culture create extraordinary outcomes for everyone they touch.

At Barnett International, a culture-first organization, he witnessed what's possible when people come first. Happy colleagues. Thriving clients. Valued partners. The impact rippled beyond the office—less stress at home, more presence with family, genuine fulfillment at work.

The lesson was clear: building great culture isn't just good for morale—it's good for business. Because business is fundamentally about people.

Matt applied this philosophy when he founded Pharmica Consulting and Intrinsic Clinical Systems—two culture-first companies where employees thrived, clients became partners, and families benefited from the joy their loved ones found at work.

After successfully exiting both companies in 2021, Matt attended the SCOPE conference, speaking with site owners, CROs, and sponsors about the future of clinical research.

Through these discussions, he heard a common concern: as sites consolidate into larger networks, they are in danger of losing their local culture, their connection with the community, and becoming disconnected from the people they serve.

"Culture isn't a nice-to-have. It's the foundation of everything great."

The Crossroads: Sites Losing Their Soul

The concern was real and widespread. Sites were being acquired at a rapid rate into larger networks. The consolidation trend was undeniable—mirroring what had already happened in the IRB and CRO industries.

But something critical was being lost in the process. The best clinical research sites have served as an extension of sponsors within their communities. They built trust with local populations. They understood cultural nuances. They maintained relationships with local physicians. They were woven into the fabric of their communities.

As consolidation accelerated, the fear was clear: sites may lose their connection to the community and their local culture as they joined larger networks. They would become too distant, too disconnected from the people they served.

The consolidation of sites is inevitable. But it doesn't have to mean sites lose their soul when they sell. Clinical research is inherently local. It needs that local culture and connection to the community to succeed.

"What if consolidation could preserve what makes each site special?"

The Biological Insight

The solution came from an unexpected place: biology.

In neuroscience, the sensorium is where all five senses integrate to create complete perception. Your eyes see color, your ears hear sound, your fingers feel texture—but it's only when these senses combine that you truly understand what you're experiencing.

The metaphor was perfect: Each clinical research site is like a sense organ—uniquely tuned to its local community, shaped by the culture it serves. One site excels at building trust in Hispanic neighborhoods. Another understands both urban and rural perspectives. A third has deep relationships with local physicians.

By emphasizing that each site keeps their local culture and connection to the community, this creates a complete understanding of diverse patient populations that would not otherwise be fully achieved.

"Local culture isn't lost in our network—it's the source of our strength."

The Sensorium Model

Sensorium Clinical Research was founded on a radical premise: sites don't have to choose between operational excellence and cultural authenticity.

The Sensorium model is different from traditional site consolidation:

  • Sites keep their name, culture, and clinical autonomy—the things that make them trusted in their communities
  • Sensorium provides the operational backbone—business development, technology, finance, HR, compliance, and patient recruitment
  • Together, we deliver better science—diverse patient populations, real-world insights, and results sponsors can trust

This isn't just a business model. It's a philosophy: culture is the foundation of great companies—and great clinical research.

Sensorium was created to be a culture-first company that respects and maintains local site culture to produce clinical excellence. A community where smaller sites can join and keep what makes them special.

This is the Sensorium Effect.

Our Mission Today

We're building a network of clinical research sites that proves you don't have to sacrifice culture for scale. Sites that maintain their local identity while delivering enterprise-grade performance. Investigators who feel supported, not managed. Patients who feel cared for, not processed.

We're proving that consolidation can be done differently—with respect for what makes each site special, with commitment to the communities they serve, and with unwavering focus on the people who make it all possible.

Most clinical trial sites scale up and lose their soul. Not here. We are different.
